The destroy being the fastest phase is the part worth noticing: taking

graded identically. That is the reconciliation proven *reproducible from
scratch*, not merely correct.

## Option 2 — the full destroy — and why it stayed out of scope

The spec's acceptance run does not distinguish layers. This one did, on purpose.

`infra/terraform/account/` holds the budgets, the Cost and Usage Report, and the
`plant-platform` Athena workgroup with its per-query scan cap — and all three
cover **WearWatch as well**. Destroying them would have removed cost guardrails
from a separate running project in order to time a rebuild here.

The CUR is the specific reason it would be a bad trade even after WearWatch is
gone: **it has no backfill.** Destroying and recreating it does not lose the
report data already written to S3, but the period it is absent is a permanent
hole in billing history, and no later apply fills it in.

What option 2 would add over what was run: it exercises the OIDC deploy role and
the CUR path, which the app layer does not touch. What it would prove is that
the *account* can be rebuilt from nothing — a real claim, and a smaller one than
the reconciliation surviving a rebuild, which is what option 1 already
demonstrated.

The judgement here is that a permanent gap in billing history is a poor price
for exercising two resources whose apply-from-zero was already evidenced in
Phase 0. Recorded as a decision rather than left as an omission.
